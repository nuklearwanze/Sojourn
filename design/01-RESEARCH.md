# SOJOURN — Part 1: Research & the Science Process

> Goal: research must *feel like research*. You cannot buy a fusion drive. You fund people and
> facilities to expand basic understanding, that understanding unlocks engineering options, and
> turning an option into flight hardware is a risky, expensive, multi-year campaign with test
> failures, overruns, dead ends, and the occasional gift from the universe.

---

## 1. The Two-Track Model

Every capability in Sojourn is produced by two interacting tracks.

### Track A — Science (understanding)
Abstracted as a graph of **Knowledge Domains** (basic + applied science). Progress is measured
in each domain as a continuous **Understanding Level (UL, 0–100)**, not boolean unlocks.
UL gates which engineering programs are *available* and sets their *risk floor*.

Domains (see 02-TECH-TREE.md for the full list) include e.g.:
Materials Science · Plasma & Nuclear Physics · Combustion & Chemical Propulsion ·
Astrodynamics & GNC · Thermal Physics · Power Systems Science · Radiation Biology ·
Closed-Ecology / Bioregenerative Science · Geosciences (per-body) · In-Situ Resource Chemistry ·
Autonomy & AI · Cryogenics · Plasma Propulsion Physics · Astrobiology · Human Factors.

You make Science progress by funding **Research Programs** that consume **Research Points (RP)**
generated by your scientists, facilities and instruments, plus by **doing missions** (flight &
surface data inject UL into the relevant domains — a Europa flyby dumps real understanding into
Geosciences:Europa and Astrobiology that no lab can buy).

### Track B — Engineering (implementation)
**Engineering Programs** turn understanding into **Technologies** (concrete, usable items:
an engine, a lander chassis, an ISRU plant, a life-support loop). Each program is a project with
scope, budget, schedule, a target **TRL**, and a **test campaign**. Programs draw on **Design
Effort (DE)** from engineers + facilities. Output is a **Technology at a Maturity Level** that
the Vehicle Designer (04-SPACEFLIGHT.md §4) can use — with reliability tied to maturity.

> Mantra: **Science says "possible." Engineering says "ready." Operations says "reliable."**

## 2. TRL: the spine of implementation

Sojourn uses the standard NASA 1–9 Technology Readiness Level scale as the literal progression
of every Engineering Program. A technology is not "researched"; it is *advanced through TRLs*.

| TRL | Meaning | In-game gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basic principles observed | Unlocked by Domain UL threshold |
| 2 | Concept formulated | Cheap paper study; produces a *design point* with wide error bars |
| 3 | Proof of concept | Lab analysis/component test; narrows performance estimate |
| 4 | Validated in lab | Breadboard; needs facility (test stand, vacuum chamber, etc.) |
| 5 | Validated in relevant environment | Subscale test in representative conditions |
| 6 | Demonstrated in relevant environment | Full prototype, ground qual or analog site |
| 7 | Demonstrated in operational environment | **Flight demo** — costs a real mission |
| 8 | Flight qualified | First operational unit completes its duty |
| 9 | Flight proven | Multiple successful uses; reliability matures |

Rules:
- A technology can only be **put on a flying vehicle at TRL ≥ 6** (with steep reliability
  penalties), comfortably at 7+, and is only "boring and dependable" at 9.
- Each TRL step has a **cost, minimum-time, and facility requirement**; you cannot buy time
  below a floor (you can parallelise programs, not compress a single test campaign past
  physical limits — captured as a soft schedule-compression penalty, "the mythical man-month").
- **Reliability** is a function of TRL + accumulated flight-units + relevant Domain UL.
  Pushing TRL-6 hardware into service is the classic risk/schedule gamble.

### Heritage & flight-proven dividend
Each successful operational use of a Technology adds **Flight Heritage**, which (a) raises
reliability asymptotically toward a tech-specific ceiling and (b) discounts derivative programs
(an uprated version of a proven engine starts partway up the TRL ladder).

## 3. How research effort is generated and spent

### Inputs
- **Scientists** (generate RP, specialised by domain) and **Engineers** (generate DE,
  specialised by discipline) — see §7 Personnel.
- **Facilities** (see 03-ECONOMY.md §7): universities/labs, test stands, wind tunnels, vacuum &
  thermal-vac chambers, centrifuges, radiation sources, neutral-buoyancy & analog sites,
  fab/clean rooms. Facilities multiply and *enable* certain TRL steps.
- **Instruments & missions**: deployed sensors and returned samples inject UL directly.
- **External knowledge**: published literature (global science tide, §5), purchased data,
  partnerships, poaching staff, espionage-lite ("open-source intelligence" only — no sabotage
  in v1).

### Allocation UI
A portfolio screen (06-UI-UX.md §4) where the player splits RP/DE budgets across active
Programs with sliders, sets priorities, and assigns named lead personnel. Over/under-staffing,
domain mismatch and facility bottlenecks are surfaced as efficiency multipliers.

### The cost curve
- Science UL has **diminishing returns** within a domain (each point above ~70 costs sharply
  more) and **synergy bonuses** across coupled domains (Plasma Physics ↔ Plasma Propulsion).
- Engineering programs have **S-curve** progress: slow start (unknown-unknowns), fast middle,
  slow validation tail.

## 4. Dead ends, overruns, failures, breakthroughs, leapfrogging

These make the tree *alive* and are seeded per game so playthroughs differ.

### 4.1 Dead ends
Some engineering approaches to a goal are, in a given game's seed, **dead ends within a TRL
band**: progress stalls, error bars refuse to shrink, costs balloon. The player gets *hints*
(rising risk index, stalled test results) before it's confirmed. Recognising and cutting a dead
end early is a skill. Mitigations: pursue **parallel approaches** to the same goal (more
expensive, de-risks), or invest in the underlying Science domain to "see further" before
committing.

> Example: pursuing a particular gas-core NTR concept may be a dead end in seed A (materials
> can't contain the plasma) while a centrifugal liquid-core variant is viable; in seed B it's
> reversed. The *category* "advanced NTR" is always reachable by *some* path.

### 4.2 Cost & schedule overruns
Every program rolls schedule/cost variance influenced by: TRL jump size, domain UL margin,
staffing quality, facility adequacy, and political interference (directed programs are
over-constrained). Overruns generate events and budget pressure. Realistic, Augustine-style.

### 4.3 Test failures (failure-that-teaches)
Test campaigns can fail. A failure costs money/schedule but **injects UL** ("we learned why the
injector screeched"). Repeated failure without UL growth ⇒ dead-end signal. Spectacular flight
failures (a TRL-7 demo RUD) also carry political/PR cost (see 05-WORLD.md §5).

### 4.4 Breakthroughs (rare, seeded + earned)
Sustained high investment in a basic-science domain accrues hidden "insight pressure"; crossing
thresholds can trigger a **Breakthrough**: a step-change that (a) sharply discounts a cluster of
techs, (b) unlocks an otherwise-locked branch early, or (c) reveals that a presumed dead end has
a hidden path. Probability scales with basic-science (not applied) investment, facility quality
and a per-game seed. Breakthroughs are *announced as discoveries* with flavour + a real-world
reference in the Sojournal. Rough cadence: a heavily-invested domain yields a meaningful
breakthrough on the order of once per ~8–15 years; rushing applied work alone almost never does.

### 4.5 Leapfrogging
Deliberate strategy: skip an intermediate generation by **over-investing in basic science** to
unlock a higher-tier branch whose prerequisites you satisfy via UL rather than via the previous
product. Costs more RP up front, risks more (you lack the intermediate's flight heritage), but
can vault ahead of competitors. The classic example: skipping solid-core NTR product lines and
going basic-plasma-physics-heavy toward NEP, or skipping early expendable heavy-lift by
over-investing in full reusability science.

### 4.6 Standing on shoulders
The **global science tide** (§5) means even unfunded domains slowly advance via the worldwide
community; you can sometimes wait and buy/license rather than fund — at the cost of competitive
lead and of others getting the flight-heritage dividend first.

## 5. The Global Science Community (the "tide")

Science is not a private resource. Every domain has a **World UL** advancing from the aggregate
investment of all factions + an exogenous baseline (real R&D continues regardless). Your private
UL is your World UL plus your lead/lag. Effects:
- Published results: factions can **publish** (boost prestige + World UL, lose exclusivity) or
  **hold/patent** (keep lead, slower World tide, licensing income). Strategic choice.
- You can **license/buy** techs others have matured, or **partner** to co-develop (share cost,
  TRL credit, and IP). See 03-ECONOMY.md §5.
- Catch-up is cheaper than frontier: trailing a domain by N levels makes your RP more efficient
  there (you're reproducing known results). This bounds runaway leads and keeps the AI relevant.

## 6. Determinism & seeding

Per-game seed fixes: which approaches are dead ends in which TRL bands; breakthrough thresholds
and which clusters they favour; the astrobiology ground-truth (05-WORLD.md §4). The *space of
possible* outcomes is fixed by the data files and physics; the *particular* outcome is the seed.
This delivers replayability without violating plausibility: every game, *some* viable path to
each capability category exists, but which path differs.

## 7. Personnel & Organisation

People are a managed resource, lightly (not an HR sim).

- **Talent pool:** scientists & engineers with discipline tags, a skill rating, a few traits
  (e.g., *Visionary* — better at low-TRL/basic; *Closer* — better at TRL 6→9 qual;
  *Maverick* — higher breakthrough chance, higher overrun variance; *Safe Hands* — reliability
  bonus). Plus **Program Managers** (schedule/cost control), **Astronauts/Operators**
  (crew-time, training pipeline), **Mission Controllers**, **Diplomats/Lobbyists**.
- **Recruitment & retention:** hire from the global pool, poach (costly, sours relations), train
  juniors over years, lose people to rivals or to morale. Brain drain is a real failure mode for
  gutted agencies and cash-strapped startups.
- **Crew pipeline (crucial for crewed play):** select → train (years, requires facilities/analog
  missions) → assign → manage **radiation dose**, **psychological load**, **bone/muscle/health**
  budgets over a career. Astronauts age out; loss-of-crew has heavy human + political cost.
- **Knowledge as an asset:** losing key personnel can *reduce* your effective UL in a niche
  domain (tacit knowledge), modelling real institutional memory loss.

## 8. Worked example (player-facing flavour)

> *Goal: a 100 kWe surface fission reactor for a lunar polar base.*
> 1. **Science:** raise Nuclear Physics UL and Power-Systems-Science UL above the program's
>    availability floor (fund reactor-physics & thermal-conversion research; a JIMO-style or
>    Kilopower-derived literature base helps).
> 2. **Engineering program "Lunar Fission 100 kWe":** start at TRL 3 (Kilopower/KRUSTY heritage
>    discounts early steps for some factions). Budget, schedule, assign a *Closer* lead +
>    nuclear engineers; requires a *Nuclear Test Facility*.
> 3. **TRL 4–5:** breadboard + non-nuclear thermal-vac sub-scale test (consumes facility time;
>    one test screeches → failure-that-teaches → +UL, +3 months).
> 4. **TRL 6:** ground qual unit (analog test at a desert site). Roll: minor overrun.
> 5. **TRL 7:** *flight demo* — fly the unit to the Moon, operate through one lunar night
>    (354 h dark). Success ⇒ TRL 8, Flight Heritage 1; now usable in base designs with rising
>    reliability. A *Breakthrough* in thermal conversion earlier would have let you leapfrog the
>    heavy radiator mass and reach this with half the radiator area.
