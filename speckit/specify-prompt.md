# `/speckit.specify` — the starter prompt

Run this in Claude Code to begin development. It describes **what** and **why** (no tech stack),
and references the design docs in `../design/` for full detail. Copy the whole block after the
command.

> Tip: after this, run `/speckit.clarify` to resolve ambiguities, then plan in slices using
> `feature-prompts.md` (the whole game is too big for one plan).

---

```
/speckit-specify Build "Sojourn", a single-player, pausable real-time hard-science 4X strategy game about the exploration, industrialisation and settlement of the Solar System from January 2026 to 2126, inspired by Aurora 4x and The Expanse. There is NO combat, NO weapons, and NO aliens; competitive pressure comes from a race for historic "firsts", economics and politics. The full design lives in the design/ folder (00-OVERVIEW, 01-RESEARCH, 02-TECH-TREE, 03-ECONOMY, 04-SPACEFLIGHT, 05-WORLD, 06-UI-UX) and the binding rules live in .specify/memory/constitution.md — treat both as authoritative source material and read them.

WHY: to let a player run a real space agency or company and make humanity multi-planetary in a way that is scientifically plausible at every step, where the felt tension is always mass, delta-v, money, crew-time, ops capacity and political capital — never invented scarcity.

WHO THE PLAYER IS: one of ten organisations, all present every game (others AI-run): national agencies NASA, ESA, Roscosmos, CSA, JAXA, and private companies Helion Launch Systems, Meridian Aerospace, Astrolith Resources, Orbital Forge, Caravel Logistics. They share one physical reality but differ in funding model (appropriation vs revenue/runway), starting assets reflecting real 2026 capabilities, political constraints, and research heritage discounts. The 2026 start has only the ISS and Tiangong, current real launch vehicles, and no established off-Earth bases. CNSA and minor agencies are always AI.

WHAT THE GAME MUST SIMULATE (functional scope):
- A deterministic, seeded, headless-capable simulation core with continuous time and event-driven time-warp that interrupts-and-pauses on things that matter (manoeuvre nodes, anomalies, reviews, budget votes, discoveries).
- Real astrodynamics: an n-body propagator with relevant perturbations as the source of truth, plus patched-conic planning aids; manoeuvre-node planning, porkchop plots, gravity-assist/flyby planning, low-thrust spiral trajectories, low-energy transfers, aerocapture/aerobraking; the rocket equation as a felt constraint. No top speed, no reactionless drives.
- A research model that is a process, not a purchase: a two-track system where funding Science raises continuous domain Understanding Levels which gate Engineering programs that advance technologies through TRL 1–9 via test campaigns, with cost/schedule uncertainty and overruns, dead ends, failures-that-teach, rare seeded breakthroughs, deliberate leapfrogging, a global science community "tide", publishing-vs-patenting, partnerships/licensing, and managed personnel (scientists, engineers, PMs, astronauts with a training/health/radiation career pipeline).
- An extensive, web-shaped tech tree across propulsion (chemical, electric/Hall/ion/MPD/VASIMR, nuclear-thermal NTP, nuclear-electric NEP, frontier fission-fragment/fusion as optional gated endgame), power & thermal, structures/materials/manufacturing & regolith construction, GNC/autonomy/comms, life support & crew health, ISRU & resource processing, launch & in-space & surface vehicles, and science/astrobiology instruments — every node carrying a real-world source. Capability categories are always reachable but specific node viability (dead ends/breakthroughs) is seeded per game.
- An economy with six currencies (funds, delta-v/propellant, mass-to-orbit, crew-time, ops capacity, political/reputation capital): agency appropriations vs private cash-runway-and-bankruptcy; resources that are addressed by location and delta-v; realistic ISRU break-even economics (lunar-ice propellant, Mars Sabatier propellant, regolith oxygen/metals, asteroid volatiles); a launch market, service-contract/RFP-bid system, partnerships/consortia, data/IP licensing, tourism and in-space manufacturing revenue; learning curves; and capital facilities (labs, test stands, pads, ground segment/DSN, stations, depots, bases).
- A logistics layer modelled as a transport graph whose nodes are dynamical locations and whose edges are window-constrained transfers priced in delta-v and time, with depots, reusable tugs and cyclers, and finite ops/comms capacity under light-time delay.
- A full Solar-System world: bodies, ~150 significant moons, and ~3,000 catalogued asteroids/comets from real orbital data plus statistical prospecting fields; per-body characterisation refined by missions (a belief-state separate from ground truth); surveyable Sites with resources/illumination/slope/hazards/comms/planetary-protection category; a COSPAR-style planetary-protection regime with forward/back-contamination consequences; and a seeded, honestly-modelled astrobiology question (possible microbial/chemical life on candidate worlds) resolved through a staged, probabilistic, mission-driven evidence process — discovered life is a science object, never an actor.
- Bases & construction: build orbital stations and surface bases from modules with emergent properties (power margin, ECLSS closure, population capacity, sustainability index), including ISRU plants and regolith-built radiation shielding; settlements can pursue self-sufficiency.
- Life support & crewed difficulty: crewed missions must be materially harder than robotic ones — consumables vs ECLSS closure fraction, radiation dose limits and solar-storm shelters, physiological deconditioning and countermeasures including artificial gravity, psychology under isolation and comms-lag, spares and maintenance, and EDL/aerocapture risk.
- A politics/events layer (no combat): faction relationships and prestige, public/political mood driving budgets and approvals, policy/treaties (launch licensing, nuclear-launch approval, planetary-protection stringency, export controls), a seeded+state-driven event system feeding the interrupt-and-pause loop, an AI world that researches/builds/flies/contracts/partners and races for milestones, and ~120 scored historic "firsts" (world-first vs faction-first) plus selectable Grand Goals (Pathfinder, Homestead, Prospector, Seeker).
- A data-dense, mostly-2D UI in the lineage of Aurora 4x and EVE Online (text and tables are first-class): a zoomable multi-focus system map, a trajectory/manoeuvre planner with porkchop plots, research & vehicle-designer screens with full traceability of every derived number, operations/fleet, economy/contracts, bases/construction, personnel, world/politics, a science-returns & astrobiology screen, a source-cited in-game encyclopedia ("Sojournal") that teaches the real science, and a configurable alerts/event log. Plan→preview→commit for irreversible actions; SI units only; accessible and keyboard-rich.

NON-FUNCTIONAL / CONSTRAINTS (see constitution): scientific plausibility with sourced data is mandatory and CI-enforced; the core must be deterministic and reproducible from seed+decisions and decoupled from the UI; content is data-driven and schema-validated; physics must validate against analytic cases (Hohmann delta-v, two-body periods, flybys); SI units only; performance must hold for 3000+ bodies and large fleets/ledgers at high time-warp with virtualised tables; deterministic round-trip-tested saves; no combat/aliens logic in v1.0 (reserved only as marked extension points).

Produce a specification covering this scope. Where the scope is too large to detail at once, organise it into clearly-bounded feature areas (sim core & time, astrodynamics, world data, vehicle designer & propulsion, research, economy & logistics, bases & construction, life support & crew, politics/events/milestones, UI) that can each be specified and planned separately, and flag ambiguities for /speckit.clarify. Do not choose a tech stack in the specification.
```
